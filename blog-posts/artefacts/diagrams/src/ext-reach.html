What a tool can reach, before and after

The old system handed a tool a bag of services and trusted it. The new one gives it exactly what its manifest declared and you approved, out of twenty-one permissions the registry recognizes.

Before — the v1 context object, handed to every tool
libraryoptional tagsoptional favoritesoptional collectionsoptional settingsoptional commandsalways present filesystemoptional, so skippable node:fsthe fallback every tool took
↓
After — Auto Tag v2 declares 7 of 21, and gets nothing else
library:read files:read tags:read tags:write settings:read embeddings:read embeddings:write library:write files:write files:copy files:rename files:delete collections:read collections:write favorites:read favorites:write desktop:reveal desktop:open drop:read drop:modify settings:write
granted to Auto Tag v2never requested, so never available

The list is not advisory

The manifest becomes a permission checker. A handler calling something it never declared throws, and the host turns that into a plain permission-denied result instead of a crash or a silent write.

Auto Tag can never delete a file

It reads your library and writes tags. It cannot copy, rename, delete, or reveal anything on disk, because it never asked for those and the host will not hand them over.

Source: ExtensionV2Permission in packages/yard-core/src/extensions-v2/definition.ts, and the Auto Tag v2 manifest in packages/yard-tools/auto-tag-v2/src/definition.ts.
